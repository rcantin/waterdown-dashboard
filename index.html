Community Safety Concerns

Issues residents and officials have raised about safety in Waterdown and Flamborough. Ward 15 ranks toward the lower end of Hamilton wards for break-ins and Flamborough often scores high on perceived safety—but property crime is above the local 5-year average, and specific corridors and incident types draw consistent concern. This is not a list of “no-go” neighbourhoods.

Vehicle theft and theft from vehicles (community-wide)

The issue mentioned most at town halls. Waterdown reported 85 stolen vehicles in 2024; mid-2025 reports were lower (~56) after initiatives such as Project Polar Bear, but still well above early-2020s levels. High-end vehicles and driveway thefts have been targets; some cases involve organized groups. Residential streets are affected across the village.

Why it matters: overnight driveway risk, insurance costs, and fear of home invasion when thieves confront residents for keys.

Hwy 6 / northern Flamborough (rural corridors)

Isolated rural properties and party venues along Hwy 6 North have been linked to serious violent incidents (including a deadly 2024 party shooting). Neighbours have called for more patrol presence. Response often starts from stations farther south until the new Hwy 6 & Parkside station opens.

Why it matters: distance from current stations, limited natural surveillance, highway access for offenders leaving a scene.

Clappison’s Corners (Hwy 5 & Hwy 6)

Long-standing conflict point for cars, trucks, and pedestrians. The signalized intersection is being replaced by an MTO interchange partly because of safety and capacity problems—high volumes, turning conflicts, and difficult crossings toward recreation facilities.

Why it matters: collision risk and barrier between neighbourhoods and community facilities.

Construction zones (Dundas St E bridge, Waterdown Road, Parkside)

Lane reductions and detours have produced illegal movements, driver confusion, and near-misses. The city has issued safety reminders. Overlapping projects increase crash and cut-through risk on local streets.

Why it matters: temporary but intense crash and pedestrian risk while major road projects run in parallel.

Residential break-ins and targeted home invasions

Ward 15 had 47 break-and-enters in 2025 (lower than many wards, but part of a suburban rise above longer-term averages). Police have warned of daytime “canvassing” crews. Incidents in newer areas (e.g. Great Falls / Mountain Brow corridor) involved confrontations for luxury vehicle keys. Commercial break-ins have also hit Waterdown business corridors.

Why it matters: loss of security at home when violence or weapons are involved.

Youth crime (Division 3 context)

HPS’s 2025 youth report showed Division 3—which includes Waterdown, Flamborough, Dundas, and Ancaster—had the highest youth-crime volume of the three divisions, shaping policing priorities across the west end.

Why it matters: school pathways, evening gatherings, and property crime involving younger offenders.

Context

A 2025 HPS community survey found Flamborough among the areas with the highest perception of neighbourhood safety city-wide, while property-crime worry remains high across Hamilton. Local concern is real—especially auto theft and response times—without meaning Waterdown ranks as Hamilton’s most dangerous place by reported volume.

Ticks in our area

Blacklegged ticks are established in southern Ontario including the Hamilton region. Remove attached ticks with fine tweezers pulling straight out; identify via eTick; seek care if symptoms develop. Prevention: long clothing, repellent, path-staying, post-outdoor checks.
