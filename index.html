Citywide pattern, 2024 → 2025

Sum of all 15 ward year-end reports — useful context so Ward 15 is not read in isolation.

  • Property crime fell in most wards: 19,047 → 17,513 (−8%).
  • Violent crime rose even as property fell: 7,150 → 7,822 (+9%).
  • Break-ins dropped almost everywhere. Theft from vehicles rose in many wards, including Ward 15.

Watch list from the reports

  • Vehicles, not homes, are the 2026 pressure. Auto theft and theft-from-auto keep showing up in early-2026 monthlies after a 2025 drop in stolen cars.
  • Quiet vs its own average. Ward 15 is 14th of 15 on volume, but 2025 property and violent totals were both above this ward’s five-year average.
  • Robbery ticked up from a small base (8 → 11). Sexual violations and break-ins moved the other way.
  • Shootings stay rare (2 in 2024, 1 in 2025). A low annual count can still include a high-harm event, as on Hwy 6 North in 2024.

How to read an HPS ward PDF

  • Page 1 — five-year annual trend for violent crime, property crime, shootings, calls for service, and collisions, plus a citywide 911 total.
  • Page 2 — year-to-date and this-month counts for assault, auto theft, break-and-enter, theft from auto, theft over, robbery, sexual violation, plus property vs violent monthly trend.
  • Page 3 — top call types, calls-for-service trend, collisions. Workload, not crime.
  • February reports are often marked N/A. HPS also reuses filenames (for example 15_jan.pdf becomes the next January), so always check the title on page 2.

How to read this dashboard

  • The indigo block is parsed from official HPS ward-report PDFs (December 2024 and 2025 year-ends, plus Ward 15 monthlies through May 2026).
  • The official live map covers only the past 60 days and five offence types, posted with a one-day delay and block-level offset.
  • Incident pins here are compiled from public HPS releases and local reporting. They are not a complete occurrence list.
  • Division 3 (Waterdown, Flamborough, Dundas, Ancaster and nearby) had the highest youth-crime volume city-wide in 2025.
